## Facts verified
- PACELC classifications and "can't achieve full consistency even if R + W > N" quoted from Abadi's paper (text extracted from the PDF): default Dynamo, Cassandra and Riak are PA/EL; VoltDB/H-Store, Megastore, BigTable/HBase are PC/EC; MongoDB PA/EC; PNUTS PC/EL.
- Facebook memcache (NSDI '13 paper text): deletes chosen over updates because deletes are idempotent; leases address stale sets and thundering herds (tokens once per 10 s per key).
- Redis: default `maxmemory-policy noeviction` (redis.conf); approximated LRU by sampling; LFU and `allkeys-*` policies (eviction docs); INCR docs describe the INCR-then-EXPIRE race and the Lua fix.
- Kafka 4.3 design docs: at-most-once/at-least-once by commit order, idempotent producer, transactions give exactly-once for Kafka-to-Kafka read-process-write with `read_committed`, "exactly-once delivery for other destination systems generally requires cooperation with such systems"; one consumer per partition per group; share groups let more consumers than partitions share records with per-record acks.
- SQS: default visibility timeout 30 s, maximum 12 hours (AWS docs).
- RFC 9110: 301 and 308 are heuristically cacheable, 302/307 aren't (only with explicit freshness); RFC 9111 `s-maxage` for shared caches; RFC 5861 `stale-while-revalidate`/`stale-if-error`. Cloudflare's default cache key includes the query string; CloudFront Origin Shield consolidates requests from regional caches.
- Cloudflare sliding-window counter: 0.003% of 400 million requests wrongly allowed or limited (Cloudflare blog). Envoy docs describe least-request P2C.
- Arithmetic in quizzes and summaries: 50M/day ÷ 86,400 ≈ 579 QPS; 100M/month ≈ 38.6 writes/s; 6 billion × 500 B = 3 TB; 62^5 ≈ 9.16 × 10^8, 62^6 ≈ 5.68 × 10^10, 62^7 ≈ 3.52 × 10^12; birthday bound ≈ √(62^7) ≈ 1.9 million; `hash % n` from 4 to 5 keeps 1/5 of keys; Amdahl 1/0.05 = 20×; 2,000 × 200 KB = 400 MB/s ≈ 3.2 Gbps; 10 replicas at 25%/25% → 13 max, 8 available (in the Docker module).
- Challenges: the consistent-hash ring reference was cross-checked against a brute-force owner search on 20,000 keys (0 mismatches); a naive linear-scan `getNode` still passes in ~0.26 s. The rate limiter's two concurrency tests fail for a GET-then-SET implementation (verified), which is the point of the challenge. Base62 encoding was fuzzed against BigInt on 200,000 random safe integers (0 mismatches), and `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` encodes to `FfGNdXsE7`.
